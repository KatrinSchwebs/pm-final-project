Does nothing: she closes the journey and means to come back (N-01).
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** Problem Hook: Our instalment journey is converting more consumers, but nearly one in five new agreements breaks within 60 days (19.0%, up from 15.2% in six months) and 31% of starters still need an agent, because consumers like Mara are asked for income and commitment before anyone tells them why, what happens if they miss a payment, or whether the plan is affordable.
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** Value Proposition: Help consumers agree to an affordable plan they can keep, without a call, while recovering more and sending agents only the cases that need them.

## Step 2 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** I opened it on my break. It asked for my income and I did not know if they meant this month or a normal month. I closed it and meant to come back.”
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Wichtigste Zahl: 1.421 von 7.364 Personen (19,3 %) verlassen das Portal bei den Einkommens- und Finanzfragen. Sie kommen nie zum Dokumentschritt.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Role: Mara is a consumer with two open claims and shift work, using the portal on her phone during a break (reconciled from N-01).

Goal: She wants to settle her open claims on terms she can afford and keep, without damaging her finances and without calling anyone.

Friction confirmed by the data

Leaving at the income and affordability step: 1,421 of 7,364 people (19.3%) never reach the document step.
Unanswered questions: missed-payment consequences (18.6% of agent contacts), combining claims (17.3%) and why income is asked (16.2%) together make up 52.1% of contacts.
Plans she can’t keep: 19.0% of agreements break within 60 days, up from 15.2%.
Several claims: agreement falls from 52.1% (one claim) to 37.7% (two) and 27.6% (three or more).
Shift income: 41.6% agree, against 48.1% for fixed salary, and 22.5% of plans break, against 17.4%.
Mobile: 43.8% agree, against 54.0% on desktop, and 853 journeys never received the income document (T-201).
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** This initiative removes the moment when Mara, on her phone with two claims and shift income, is asked for her income without being told why, what a missed payment would mean, or how her claims fit together, and so leaves or guesses a plan she can’t keep.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Expected behaviour change: Mara finishes the income step instead of leaving, picks a plan she can afford instead of guessing, and handles two claims in one plan without calling. These are hypotheses to test, not results.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Leading indicator: the share of consumers who start the affordability questions and go on to the next step. Today that is 80.7% (5,943 of 7,364), so 19.3% leave. The gap is closing when that share rises.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Guardrail metric: plan-kept rate. This is the share of new agreements with no missed payment within 60 days. It is 81.0% today (3,115 of 3,846).
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Minimum to proceed (to scale): the leading indicator rises by at least 3 points (80.7% → 83.7% or more), plan-kept stays at or above 78%, and the agreement rate stays inside its six-month range (43.1–46.3%). The pack sets no targets, so these thresholds are my proposals for you to confirm.
