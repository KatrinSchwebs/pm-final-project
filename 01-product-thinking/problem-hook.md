## Responses
- **Chosen path, which Riverty business-unit case are you using?:** Business Unit collection
- **Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:** What happens over 12 months

Months 0 to 3: nothing visible. Results look like business as usual. The cost is invisible: consumers abandon, plans fail, agents repeat checks, and nobody can see how much because plan quality isn’t measured.

Months 3 to 6: costs surface internally. Cost to serve rises with volume because the agent work doesn’t shrink. Leadership’s SSR target is missed or, worse, met through easy cases while hard instalment cases stay with agents.

Months 6 to 12: the market feels it.

Client conversations get harder. Clients compare providers on recovery, reputation and reporting. Riverty has weaker evidence on all three and risks losing mandates or new business to providers that can show digital, transparent journeys.
Purchased portfolios underperform. Where Riverty carries the risk itself, lower recovery and higher servicing cost hit its own returns, which limits how much it can bid for future portfolios.
Expansion slows. Without reusable journeys, each new market means another custom build.
Compliance exposure grows. Inconsistent treatment of vulnerable consumers and uneven affordability handling are weaker positions as scrutiny increases.
Net effect on market position

Under these assumptions, Riverty doesn’t collapse. It erodes: it becomes a more expensive, less transparent option in a market that is moving toward digital self-service. The damage is mostly in what it can’t win rather than what it loses, such as new clients, portfolio bids and market launches. A shock is possible, for example a major client moving volume or a regulatory finding, but that would be a trigger, not the baseline.

- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** Consumers like Mara who can’t pay in full abandon the instalment journey or sign up for plans they can’t keep, so Riverty loses recoverable money, agents absorb the cleanup, and a rising SSR can hide the damage because it counts a plan as resolved the moment it is created.
- **Value proposition, based on the initiative in your assigned Riverty case, what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** The initiative: a digital instalment journey in the German pilot, with the portal as the entry point and a clear handoff to an agent. It covers single and multiple claims for one consumer segment, and is built so it can extend to other markets later.

The new value:

For	New value
Consumers like Mara	A plan she can understand, afford and keep, with answers to her questions on the screen: why we ask about income, what happens if she misses a payment, and how two claims fit together. She can finish on her phone without calling.
Agents like Daniel	Cases arrive with a summary of what the consumer entered, why a case was escalated, and how current the data is. He stops repeating checks and has an easy way to correct a bad result.
Clients	Responsible, transparent recovery with reporting they can trust, which protects their reputation.
Riverty (purchased portfolios)	Better recovery and lower servicing cost on receivables where Riverty carries the risk.
Leadership	An SSR that reflects outcomes, because plan-kept rate sits next to it instead of counting a plan as resolved the moment it is created.

One-line version: help consumers agree to an affordable plan they can keep, without a call, while recovering more and sending agents only the cases that need them.

Why launch now

The brief gives no deadline or market data, so these reasons rest on assumptions:

The cost is hidden and ongoing. Abandonment, incomplete data and missed plans happen every month, and today nobody can measure how much, because plan quality isn’t tracked.
The north star can mislead. Leadership is pushing SSR, which rewards creating plans. Starting without a quality measure risks improving the number while outcomes worsen. A pilot with plan-kept rate built in fixes that early.
A small pilot is cheap compared with waiting. One country, one segment and a human handoff limit the risk, and 12 months of inaction means 12 months without evidence.
Scale depends on it. Each new market adds cost unless the reusable journey is proven first, and expansion should wait for pilot evidence, not precede it.
Expectations are rising (assumption). Clients and regulators increasingly expect digital, transparent and fair servicing, and the window to build this before it becomes a requirement is limited.

## Cold-read your own hook
- **Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?:** A skeptical stakeholder would ask:

How much money? “Recoverable money” has no number attached.
How many consumers? “Like Mara” could be 2% of cases or 40%.
Is this new? Collections have always had abandonment and failed plans. Why now?
Compared with what? The sentence has no baseline, benchmark or cost.
- **Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?:** The journey is fragmented across portal, letters, reminders, decision logic and agent processes. That is a design property, so it affects everyone who enters it.
It names three recurring outcomes: abandonment, incomplete affordability information and missed plans. Those are different symptoms, but they come from the same causes, which are unclear explanations and a hard-to-complete flow.
Agents “pick up the pieces,” which implies the failure repeats often enough to be a workload.
Mara’s questions (is it affordable, why my income, what if I miss, can both claims go together, could it make things worse) are ones any consumer who can’t pay in full would plausibly have. They come from the situation, not from her personally.
- **Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle you identified, or is it a generic feature?:** Verdict: partly. The value proposition names the right obstacles, but much of it is outcome language that any digital instalment tool could claim. It only removes the obstacle where it commits to a specific mechanism, and several of those are missing.
